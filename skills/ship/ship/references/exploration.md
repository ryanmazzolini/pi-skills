# Explore Before Converging

Use this loop when the starting frame, assumptions, or candidate space may be incomplete. A narrow lookup or experiment with one clear hypothesis does not need it.

## Orient around a provisional question

State:

- the question and the decision or understanding it could affect
- observed facts, current assumptions, and terms that may hide disagreement
- constraints and what evidence could change the answer or reframe the question
- the checkpoint to resume when the investigation returns

Investigation can reduce uncertainty about the frame, evidence, causal model, or available approaches. Send goals and value trade-offs that evidence cannot settle to Align. Send a question that requires human use or observation to Prototype.

## Survey independent avenues

Choose enough independent avenues to expose a weak frame without turning them into a checklist:

- **System:** code, docs, tests, history, issues, observed behavior, and operational evidence.
- **Topic:** varied terminology across authoritative documentation, standards, literature, and the relevant ecosystem.
- **Lineage:** backward references, forward citations, later replications or criticism, and related work from demonstrably central authors or maintainers.
- **Neighborhood:** related or co-cited work, adjacent fields, and mechanisms that might transfer across domains.
- **Challenge:** contrary results, failure reports, limitations, competing explanations, and viable alternatives.
- **Experiment:** a benchmark, technical spike, or experiential prototype when documents cannot settle the uncertainty.

For an ordinary technical decision, start with 2–4 genuinely different topic queries, 2–4 strong seed artifacts, one lineage pass on the most important seeds, and one challenge or adjacent-domain pass. Skip this quota when local evidence or one authoritative source settles a narrow question. Expand it only when the decision's consequence and remaining uncertainty justify the cost.

Citation and related-author searches supplement topic search; they do not prove completeness. Use more than one seed when practical because these searches inherit the seed's vocabulary, citation network, popularity, language, and disciplinary biases.

## Traverse original artifacts

Use the model to locate terminology, authors, artifacts, and possible connections. Then verify titles, authors, links, dates, quotations, and important claims in the original sources or live system.

Keep candidate discovery separate from evidence acceptance. An artifact becomes evidence because its method, authority, direct observation, or implementation is relevant—not because the model recommended it or its author is prominent.

Follow another reference, citation, author, or neighboring concept when it could add a new concept, contradiction, method, or viable option. Preserve materially different candidate families before elaborating a favorite.

## Sift separately

Compare candidates on dimensions that matter to the question, such as evidentiary strength, relevance, feasibility, reversibility, differentiation, and testability. Look explicitly for evidence that would falsify the emerging view and for parts that can be subtracted.

When comparison itself supplies the evidence, evaluate alternatives at comparable fidelity before iterating one. Use independent judgment for consequential selection when it is worth the coordination cost.

## Stop and return

Stop when the evidence supports a next action, material disagreement and gaps are explicit, and another pass is unlikely to change the frame or recommendation. For citation expansion, one iteration that adds no decision-relevant concept, contradiction, method, or option is a practical stopping signal; it indicates diminishing returns, not proven completeness.

Return:

- whether the starting question was answered, reframed, or rejected
- the recommendation or map of viable directions
- the strongest evidence and material counterevidence
- remaining assumptions and uncertainty
- the exact Ship, Align, or Prototype checkpoint to resume
